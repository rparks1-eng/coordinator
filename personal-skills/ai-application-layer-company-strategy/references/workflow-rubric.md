# Discovery and evaluation rubric

Score each candidate workflow from 0–2 on urgency, frequency, cost of current workaround, buyer access, buyer authority, representative-case access, permitted data use, measurable outcome, reversible pilot, and unacceptable-failure clarity. A score is a discovery aid, not proof of demand.

For an agent comparison, fix task cases, model/provider, allowed tools, cost cap, and outcome grader. Compare fixed and constrained designs on successful outcome, cost, latency, error/rework, override, and incidents. Add autonomy only when it improves the customer-visible outcome at an acceptable boundary.

Counterexamples: infrastructure or model differentiation can be the company when the differentiated value is training data, systems efficiency, specialized deployment, performance, or platform distribution. An application is shallow when it has no workflow access, distribution, feedback loop, trust, or economic benefit.
